# Home-screen image prompts

These prompts use polished healthcare concept renders to make two future
workflows visibly different at thumbnail size. They draw on the supplied Philips
guidance for warm clinical lighting, uncluttered composition, approachable human
presence, and restrained Philips Blue accents. Concept rendering is a deliberate
departure from the guidance's default preference for photography, to communicate
speculative experiences. These are not formally Philips-approved assets or claims
about existing products. Generated assets need brand, clinical accuracy, and
usage-rights review before external publication. Keep official logos out of
generated images.

Generate each image separately in ChatGPT. Request landscape 16:9 PNG files,
ideally 2048 x 1152 pixels. Do not generate a button or website screenshot.
Generate Near Future first, then attach that image when requesting Distant Future
to keep the pair consistent. The difference must be visible without reading labels:
Near Future shows hands-on control of coordinated imaging; Distant Future shows
one integrated vessel-planning workspace, with fewer separate controls.

## Near Future

```text
Create a polished, realistic industrial-design concept render for a Near Future
IVUS workflow entry tile. Landscape 16:9, 2048 x 1152 pixels. This is a designed
future-workflow visualization, not a generic hospital photograph.

Show an achievable next step in image-guided vascular care: a clinician in teal
scrubs actively touching a table-side touchscreen, with coordinated IVUS and
X-ray on a large physical display directly behind it. Use a close three-quarter,
over-the-shoulder composition. The clinician's hand and touchscreen are clear
foreground elements; the main imaging display fills the middle of the frame.
Keep the clinician's face out of view. The two physical screens and the direct
touch interaction must remain recognizable when reduced to a small thumbnail.

On the main display, show clearly separated but coordinated views: a grayscale
IVUS vessel cross-section, a longitudinal pullback strip, and grayscale vascular
X-ray with one restrained blue location marker linking the views. On the
touchscreen, show a simple matching pullback strip with a few large controls,
without text. Keep imaging plausible and schematic, with no diagnostic claims.
A familiar ceiling-mounted C-arm can appear subtly in the background, but the
room and equipment are supporting context, not the subject. No patient.

Use premium product-visualization materials, crisp readable displays, soft warm
clinical lighting, neutral white and light-gray equipment, restrained Philips
Blue (#0B5ED7), and pale blue (#BDF0FF). Human-centered, practical and refined.
Use moderate depth of field without blurring the imaging or touch interaction.
Keep the key elements inside the central 80% of the frame for safe cropping.

Make this unmistakably NEAR FUTURE through familiar equipment and hands-on
touchscreen control, not a science-fiction room. Do not make a large 3D vessel
model the focal point; reserve that visual language for the Distant Future pair.
No embedded text, letters, numbers, logos, watermarks, patient identifiers,
identifiable faces, holograms, floating screens, neon effects or branded product
claims. Return only the rendered image, not a website or button mockup.
```

## Distant Future

```text
Create a polished, realistic industrial-design concept render for a Distant
Future IVUS workflow entry tile. Landscape 16:9, 2048 x 1152 pixels. Use the
attached Near Future image as a reference for lighting, materials, palette and
render quality, but NOT for the workflow or screen composition.

Show a more transformative, speculative image-guided vascular-care experience:
a clinician working with one unified, patient-specific 3D vessel-planning
workspace on a large, physically mounted display. Use synthetic anatomy only.
The display occupies roughly two-thirds of the composition and is the dominant
subject. Show the clinician from behind at one side, naturally pointing toward
a treatment-planning location, without obscuring the vessel map. Keep their face
out of view. Use a close three-quarter composition, not a wide room photograph.

The display centers a large, elegant, anatomically plausible 3D peripheral
vessel reconstruction in pale blue, with one short selected treatment segment
in restrained aqua. Integrate a small grayscale IVUS cross-section at the
corresponding location, a subtle measurement bracket, and a translucent planned
treatment zone along the vessel. These elements form ONE spatially coherent
workspace, not a grid of separate monitors or unrelated windows. No text or
numerical labels. The 3D vessel and treatment zone must be immediately legible
at thumbnail size. This is a speculative planning concept, not a claim of
clinical capability, autonomous diagnosis or automatic treatment.

Show fewer separate consoles and controls than in the Near Future image. Keep
physical equipment understated and plausible; any C-arm or procedure table is
only supporting background context. No patient. Communicate the future through
integrated information and a changed clinician interaction, not exotic hardware.

Match the Near Future image's premium product-visualization materials, soft warm
clinical lighting, neutral white and light-gray equipment, restrained Philips
Blue (#0B5ED7), and pale blue (#BDF0FF). Calm, human-centered and refined. Keep
the clinician and main visualization inside the central 80% for safe cropping.

Make this unmistakably DISTANT FUTURE: one dominant 3D vessel-planning view,
integrated imaging and treatment context, and fewer separate controls. Do not
repeat the Near Future touchscreen-and-separate-imaging-panel composition.
No embedded text, letters, numbers, logos, watermarks, patient identifiers,
identifiable faces, holograms, floating screens, neon effects or implausible
robotic hardware. Return only the rendered image, not a website or button mockup.
```

## Reference

Philips Design Language skill supplied in this workspace session: Imagery
(photography priority, composition, lighting, tone), Color Palette
(Philips Blue and Light Blue), and Logo (use official logo assets).
The two concept directions reflect the user's agreement to hands-on coordinated
imaging for Near Future and a unified 3D vessel-planning experience for Distant
Future. They are creative briefs, not specifications of the implemented prototypes.
