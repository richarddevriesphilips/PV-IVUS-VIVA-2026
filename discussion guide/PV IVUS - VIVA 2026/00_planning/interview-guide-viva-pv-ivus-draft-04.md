# VIVA · Both prototypes and feature ranking

**Draft 04 · 23 September 2026 · AI-generated · For feedback**

Both receive explicit feedback questions below; deployment has protected discussion time.

## What we want to learn

1. How participants understand and navigate the near-future and distant-future workflows.
2. How participants interpret automated lumen and vessel measurements, what they would verify, and how they would handle a result they disagree with.
3. How deploy mode fits the transition from planning to deployment: what must be visible, who operates it, and what information or controls are missing.
4. Which features add the most value to their own practice, and which additions are worth pursuing sooner versus later.

This is a rapid comparative concept evaluation with brief hands-on use. Within ten minutes, the run-throughs must follow a prepared, short route through each prototype. Record hesitation and confusion, but do not interpret a guided run-through as a full independent usability test.

## Prototype run-throughs

Use the same clinically reviewed illustrative case and equivalent starting points in both versions. The participant holds the mouse or uses the intended input device. Start from a prepared pullback instead of spending the session recording one.

### Near Future · 1½ minutes

**Proposed route:** Review a point of interest → relate IVUS to X-ray → define a segment → interpret length.

**Main task:** “These are the two agreed endpoints for this example. Show me how you would review the region and capture the segment you want to assess.”

If time permits, ask: **“What would this change about how you work today?”** Move to Distant Future at 3:00; do not repeat the full shared workflow there.

Observe what they try first, whether they understand image linkage and length information, and any place they need help. Avoid naming the control before the first attempt. If an interaction stalls for about 15–20 seconds, note it and give a minimal cue so the run-through can continue; record the cue.

### Distant Future · 3½ minutes, including dedicated deploy-mode questions

**Confirmed differences:** Automated lumen and vessel measurements, plus deploy mode. Do not conflate cross-sectional lumen/vessel measurements with longitudinal segment length. Automation alone does not establish that the feature uses AI.

#### 3:00–4:00 · Automated lumen and vessel measurements

Start at the same region reviewed in Near Future, with the automated measurements visible.

**Task and core question:** “Review the lumen and vessel measurements shown here. What would you check before using them?”

**Short follow-up:** “If you disagreed with a measurement, what would you want to do?”

Observe whether they distinguish lumen from vessel measurements, understand the displayed values, and seek supporting image information. Capture desired verification and correction behavior. Do not promise that editing, overriding, or confidence indicators are implemented; record these as expectations if absent.

#### 4:00–6:30 · Deploy mode: hands-on use and feedback

**4:00–5:00 — Task:** “Planning is complete for this example. Show me how you would prepare the view for deployment, using the information you have selected.”

Let the participant enter deploy mode and work with the available controls. If they cannot find the mode, record the difficulty and point them to it so they can still give feedback. Ask them to describe what they expect as they act. Keep the screen visible during the questions.

**5:00–6:30 — Ask all three core questions, inviting brief answers:**

1. **Information and clarity:** “Looking at this deploy view, what information is missing, and what would you remove or make more prominent?”
2. **Workflow and control:** “When would you switch into this mode, and who in your team would operate it?”
3. **Value and concerns:** “Compared with how you deploy today, what would this mode change, if anything? What concerns would you have?”

**Optional probes — use only to clarify an answer, not as extra required questions:**

- “What do you think these markers refer to, and how would you check their position?”
- “What should happen to the planning information when it appears in the live view?”
- “If a marker obscures something you need to see, what would you expect to do? How would you bring it back?”
- “If you needed to change the plan during deployment, how would you expect to return to it?”
- “When would this mode be unnecessary or get in your way?”

**Observe and capture:** Entry into deploy mode; live versus planning interpretation; marker meaning; image visibility and clutter; missing information; operator/handoff; expected return to planning; one benefit or limitation grounded in their current workflow. Record what they actually did separately from what they said they would want.

## Feature-ranking exercise · 3 minutes

The sketch appears to build from IVUS alone through X-ray, length measurement, bookmarks/segments, deployment, and AI assistance. Use **IVUS alone as the reference**, then rank the added capabilities individually. Ranking cumulative bundles would make it difficult to tell which addition drove the preference.

Prepare five core cards of equal size. The automated-measurement card replaces the earlier broad AI card so ranking reflects the specific feature participants encounter. The IDs below are for note-taking, not an intended priority order. Shuffle their starting positions between participants. Keep roadmap dates and Near/Distant labels off the cards during the initial ranking.

| ID | Card label | Neutral description |
|---|---|---|
| F1 | IVUS + X-ray review | Review IVUS with corresponding recorded X-ray information to relate a finding to its location. |
| F2 | Segment length | Define two endpoints and view the length between them when available. |
| F3 | Bookmarks and segments on X-ray | Mark and revisit selected findings or segments in their X-ray context. |
| F4 | Deploy mode | Use a dedicated deployment view with relevant planning information alongside live X-ray. |
| F5 | Automated lumen and vessel measurements | View automatically generated lumen and vessel measurements on the IVUS image. |

The card descriptions combine the sketch with the researcher’s clarified prototype scope. Separate **selecting and revisiting information** (F3) from **using it during deployment** (F4), and **segment length** (F2) from **lumen/vessel measurements** (F5). Record if the participant values lumen and vessel automation differently, without adding a second ranking exercise.

The sketch’s broader AI-assistance idea remains a future topic, distinct from the confirmed automated measurements. If a separate AI capability is intended for ranking, define it concretely and add a sixth card before piloting; do not assume automated measurements and unspecified AI assistance are the same feature.

### 6:30–7:00 · Establish the cards

Lay out the cards and briefly identify each capability. Distinguish what they used, what they only saw, and what is hypothetical. Do not claim savings, accuracy, safety, or clinical benefit.

**Say:** “Thinking about your own cases, rank these additions by the value they would add compared with IVUS alone. Assume they work as described; we’re asking about value, not how difficult they are to build.”

### 7:00–8:00 · Rank

Ask the participant to arrange the cards from most to least valuable. Provide two separate parking areas: **Not relevant to my work** and **Not enough information**. Parked cards are not automatically assigned the lowest rank.

Encourage a relative order, but record a genuine tie rather than inventing a distinction. Note any feature they consider a prerequisite for another; dependencies should not be confused with greater standalone value.

### 8:00–9:00 · Explain the priorities

**Main probe:** “Why is this your number one? Where would it have made a difference in a recent case?”

**Short follow-up:** “What put deploy mode in that position?” If deploy mode was already discussed as their top choice, ask why automated lumen/vessel measurements sit where they do. This ensures the ranking captures a reason for deployment priority, not just its position.

Capture the reason, not just the order. If the explanation reveals a misunderstanding, clarify neutrally and record any revised ranking.

### 9:00–9:30 · Place the first investment

**Ask:** “Which one should we work on next, and which could wait?”

Mark **Next** and **Later** on their choices without rearranging the value ranking. This preserves the sketch's near-term versus longer-term discussion and shows when the preferred next investment differs from the most valuable feature. It does not imply a delivery promise.

## How to use the results

Summarize feature rankings with exposure denominators, top-choice counts, and the reasons behind them. Report parked cards and ties separately. Keep participant-level orders available and examine differences by role and IVUS experience; a small conference sample cannot support market-wide conclusions.

Report prototype observations alongside priorities. A high-ranked feature may still have an unclear interaction; an easy interaction may have little perceived value. Separate hands-on observations, reactions to static concepts, and expectations about automated measurements. Keep deploy-mode feedback explicit in the findings: information needs, visibility, workflow timing, operator, concerns, and ranked priority. Retain both **value priority** and **work-on-next priority** rather than collapsing them into a composite score.
